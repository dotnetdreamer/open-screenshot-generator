# Third party assets: provenance and redistribution

**The code in this repository is MIT licensed. The bundled artwork is not.**

The [MIT licence](https://github.com/dotnetdreamer/open-screenshot-generator/blob/main/LICENSE)
covers the source: the editor, the CLI, the desktop shell, the scripts, the
templates' JSON. It does **not** cover the image files those templates paint.
Those are licensed separately, on terms that are not MIT and that do not permit
everything MIT permits. Copying this repository does not transfer them to you.

This file is the record of what is in here, where it came from, and what the
published npm package does about it. It is a factual record, not legal advice.

## What is in the repository

`public/` is about 176 MB. Four areas, with different provenance and different
answers.

| Area | Size | Contents | Provenance record |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `public/elements/images/` | 55 MB, 62 files | The Images palette: cut out photographs of hands, people, food and study scenes, plus the store badges | Complete. [docs/image-asset-licenses.md](https://github.com/dotnetdreamer/open-screenshot-generator/blob/main/docs/image-asset-licenses.md) |
| `public/elements/device-3d/` | 22 MB, 156 files | Thumbnails for the 3D device poses and colour presets in the palette | In house. Rendered from the app's own device components by this repository's regeneration pass |
| `public/data/projects/` | 100 MB, 101 template JSON plus 363 PNG | Template artwork: photographic art baked into templates, placeholder app screens, and one preview PNG per template | **Partial.** Nine photographs are recorded. The rest is not. See below |
| `public/data/ai/` | small | The generated template catalog, `catalog.txt` | In house, generated by `npm run gen:ai-catalog` |

## 1. `public/elements/images/`: recorded, and not redistributable on its own

[docs/image-asset-licenses.md](https://github.com/dotnetdreamer/open-screenshot-generator/blob/main/docs/image-asset-licenses.md)
is the authoritative record for this folder. In summary, and repeated here
because it is the part that governs redistribution:

- Every photograph in the Images palette is from the **Adobe Stock free
  collection**, licensed on **6 July 2026** (with later additions dated in the
  record) under the **Adobe Stock standard licence**, through the project owner's
  Adobe account. The licence history is held by Adobe.
- Filenames embed the Adobe Stock asset id (`<group>-as<ID>.png`), so every file
  traces back to its Stock page.
- The licence is **held by this project**. It is **not sublicensable**. Cloning
  this repository does not license those images to you, and the MIT text in
  `LICENSE` does not do it either.
- The standard licence permits worldwide, perpetual commercial use of the images
  **inside composite works**, which is what an exported store screenshot is.
  Attribution is not required for that use.
- The standard licence does **not** permit distributing a Stock asset in a way
  whose primary purpose is for third parties to extract it as a standalone file.
  These files exist so the editor can composite them. A zip of them, a CDN of
  them, or a release asset containing them would be a different thing.
- The store badges (`badges/`) are the genuine badges each vendor publishes for
  promoting app availability, downloaded from each vendor's own badge programme.
  Each vendor's guidelines govern their use, and those guidelines generally
  require the badge unmodified, with clear space, to indicate real availability.

The record names every file, its Stock id and its title. Keep it current: an
asset added without a row in that table is an asset nobody can account for later.

## 2. `public/data/projects/`: incomplete, and it needs finishing

This is the gap.

Section 4 of
[docs/image-asset-licenses.md](https://github.com/dotnetdreamer/open-screenshot-generator/blob/main/docs/image-asset-licenses.md)
records **nine** photographs baked into templates (the Feature Graphic paw shot,
six Calora food and gym cutouts, two Amoura coffee shots), all Adobe Stock
standard licence through the same account, and states that the `fg-screens/*.png`
skeleton screens are original in house placeholders.

That leaves the rest of the folder without a written record:

- the per template preview PNGs in `previews/` (101 files, 38 MB),
- the placeholder app screens in `app-screens/`, `mac-screens/` and
  `watch-screens/` (150 files), which are believed to be generated in house by
  `scripts/gen-app-skeletons.js`, `gen-mac-skeletons.js` and
  `gen-watch-skeletons.js`,
- the remaining root level PNGs used directly by templates (about 98 files),
  which include app screen art, background plates and decorative pieces.

Most of that is very likely original work by this project, and the project's own
asset policy has been "original only, no extraction and no downloads" since it
was written down. But "very likely" is not a record. **Each of those files needs
one line saying who made it and under what terms**, in the same table form as the
images record, before anyone should treat the folder as safe to redistribute.

Until that exists, treat `public/data/projects/` the same way this file treats
`public/elements/images/`: fine to render from, not to be republished as a
standalone artwork download.

What a row needs, at minimum: the file, who or what produced it, the date, and
the licence or the internal note "original work by this project". For anything
generated by a script, name the script. For anything derived from a third party
asset, name the asset and its licence, the way the Stock rows do.

## 3. What the npm package ships

The rule the published `open-screenshot-generator` tarball follows, and the
reason `cli/editor/assets.ts` exists at all:

- **The tarball ships the program, not the artwork.** That is the Next.js app
  shell, the 101 template JSON documents, and the generated AI catalog. Together
  a few megabytes.
- **No image file that this project does not own outright is in the tarball.**
  Not the Images palette, not the template photographs, not the previews.
- **Artwork is fetched at run time from the project's own deployment**, on first
  use, per file, over plain HTTPS. That request is byte for byte the request a
  browser makes when a person visits the site and opens the same template. The
  CLI is a headless browser pointed at the same origin; it is not a mirror and it
  is not a downloader.
- **It is verified and cached per machine.** Each file is checked against a
  sha256 in the packaged `assets.manifest.json` and written into a content
  addressed cache under the OS cache directory. A digest mismatch is a hard
  failure, never a silent substitution.
- **No artwork archive is published anywhere.** Not as a GitHub release asset,
  not as a separate npm package, not as a downloadable zip, not behind a CLI
  subcommand that assembles one. `osg cache warm` populates a local cache from
  the live site, and `osg cache warm --from ./public` copies from a checkout the
  user already has. Neither creates a distributable bundle.

If that rule is ever relaxed, that is the moment to get the legal read, not
after. Packaging artwork into a downloadable artefact is exactly the use the
Adobe Stock standard licence carves out, and it changes the question for every
other file in `public/` at the same time.

## 4. If you fork this

The code is yours under MIT. The artwork is not, and the fork does not inherit
the licences.

Practically:

- **You may run it.** Cloning, building and using the editor for your own store
  graphics is what the assets were licensed for. Exports you produce are
  composite works and the standard licence covers them.
- **You may not republish the artwork.** Do not publish `public/elements/images/`
  or the recorded template photographs to a package registry, a CDN, a release
  asset, a dataset or a mirror. Do not point a "download all assets" feature at
  them.
- **Your deployment is your responsibility.** If you deploy your fork publicly
  and it serves those files, you are distributing them under a licence held by
  someone else. Either license your own copies through your own Adobe account and
  replace the files, or remove them. Removing one is two edits: delete the file
  from `public/elements/images/<group>/` and its entry in
  [src/lib/imageLibrary.ts](https://github.com/dotnetdreamer/open-screenshot-generator/blob/main/src/lib/imageLibrary.ts).
- **Point the CLI at your own origin.** `assetsBaseUrl` in `osg.config.ts`, or
  `--assets-base-url`, decides where artwork is hydrated from. A fork that hosts
  its own artwork sets that and stops depending on this project's deployment.
- **Store badges follow the vendors, not us.** Apple's, Google's, Microsoft's,
  Amazon's and F-Droid's badge guidelines govern those files wherever they end
  up.
- **Keep the record.** If you add assets, add rows. A fork with no provenance
  record is a fork that cannot answer a takedown.

## 5. Scope and disclaimer

This file records what the project knows about the provenance of the files it
ships and the rule the published package follows. It is written by the
maintainers and it is **not legal advice**. It does not create a licence, and
nothing in it grants a right the underlying licences do not already grant.

Two specific recommendations, stated plainly:

1. **Finish the `public/data/projects/` record** before anyone relies on this
   file as complete.
2. **Get a lawyer to read the Adobe Stock terms** against any plan to package
   artwork into a downloadable artefact, before that artefact is built, published
   or announced. The relevant terms are at
   <https://stock.adobe.com/license-terms>.
